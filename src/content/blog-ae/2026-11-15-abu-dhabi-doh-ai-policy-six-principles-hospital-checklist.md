If your hospital is licensed by the Abu Dhabi Department of Health (DoH) and uses AI in any form, one short document applies to you: the Policy on Use of Artificial Intelligence (AI) in the Healthcare Sector of the Emirate of Abu Dhabi. It is version 0.9, dated 30 April 2018, and its effective date is the date of publication. It is only a few pages long, but it sets the tone for how AI is expected to be run. The sections below give the parts an administrator or nurse leader can act on.

## Who it covers

The policy applies to DoH-licensed healthcare providers, UAE-based pharmaceutical manufacturers, healthcare insurers, licensed researchers involved in human research, and every local, national or locally based international end user that uses Abu Dhabi population or patient clinical and non-clinical data in AI work, directly or indirectly. In plain terms: if your tool uses Abu Dhabi patient data, the policy is in the room, whoever built the tool.

## The six guiding principles

The policy names six principles: transparency (AI software should be verifiable and explainable), user assistance or supportive technology, safety and security, privacy, ethics (human dignity and autonomy), and accountability of researchers and developers to users. The second one matters most day to day. The policy defines supportive technology as technology that assists a human decision and does not replace it, so a human makes the final decision and is responsible for the action taken. A discharge-summary drafter, a risk score or a scheduling tool is a helper, not the person in charge.

## What it asks of the hospital

Healthcare end users other than patients should:

- have clear governance on AI use;
- state where AI enters the process and what the role of the physician or practitioner is;
- set boundaries on access to and sharing of patient information;
- educate everyone involved, including patients;
- audit AI functionality regularly and report to DoH as needed; and
- comply with national and DoH rules on e-health, health information exchange, data protection and information security.

They must also report all known or suspected AI incidents or deficiencies, and any implementation issue that could affect patient safety.

## What it asks of the tool

For AI tools, the minimum requirements include certification of the software and of upgraded major versions from recognised international agencies, compliance with ADSSSA regulations, and auditable validation statements. Tools should have “graceful degradation”, meaning automatic alerts and gradual cessation if hardware or software malfunctions, and should go through repeated improvement cycles based on end-user feedback sent to the DoH Drug and Medical Products Regulation Department and the manufacturer. They should be subject to audits and comply with DoH’s Health Information Exchange policy.

## A one-page checklist to build from it

1. Name who owns AI governance in your hospital.
2. List each AI tool, where it enters the process and who makes the final decision.
3. Ask each vendor for its certification and validation statements, in writing.
4. Ask what the tool does when it fails (graceful degradation).
5. Decide who reports incidents to DoH, and by what route.
6. Plan patient information and staff training.

## Key takeaways

- Abu Dhabi’s AI policy applies to any DoH-licensed provider that uses Abu Dhabi patient data in AI work.
- Its central idea: AI supports a human decision, and a human stays responsible.
- Hospitals are asked for governance, clear roles, data boundaries, education, audits and incident reporting; tools are asked for certification, validation statements and graceful degradation.
- This is general information, not legal advice. Read the policy itself and ask your compliance officer before acting.
