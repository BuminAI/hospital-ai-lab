When a vendor presents an AI product to a Dubai hospital, the questions that matter are rarely on the slides. The Dubai Health Authority (DHA) policy “Artificial Intelligence in the Healthcare” (reference DHA/HISHD/PP-09) turns many of them into a list. Its control table gives an issue date of 1 July 2021, an effective date of 1 October 2021 and a revision date of 1 July 2026, so check DHA for a newer version before you rely on this summary.

## Who it covers

The policy applies to DHA-licensed facilities and professionals that use AI, to national and locally based international AI developers that use Dubai data, and to UAE-based pharmaceutical manufacturers, health insurers, public health entities and researchers using AI in Dubai.

## What a vendor must disclose

The policy says the disclosure about an AI solution must cover:

- which functions are AI-enabled;
- how the solution was validated;
- which data sets were or must be used;
- the expected outcomes;
- the healthcare professional’s role in the final decision;
- the type of algorithm;
- the ethical examination of how data is used;
- how performance must be validated;
- how the solution is integrated into care; and
- the limits of the data and algorithms.

You can turn that list straight into a vendor questionnaire. A vendor who cannot answer “what are the limits of your data?” in writing has told you something useful.

## Independent validation

All AI solutions for healthcare must also go through independent validation by a competent third party. A vendor’s own validation study is the start of the conversation, not the end of it.

## Oversight after go-live

AI solutions with a significant effect on people’s lives must be designed so that designated end users can override them or reverse their decisions, and they must have built-in appeals procedures. Performance must be monitored in clinical practice and actual outcomes compared with those from training and validation; significant gaps lead to further inspection. End users must report incidents and patient-safety issues to DHA’s Health Regulation Sector and have AI functionality audited regularly by an independent third party.

## What a nurse leader can ask

- Who can override this tool on the ward, and how?
- What will we compare real results against?
- Who do staff tell when something looks wrong?

## Key takeaways

- Dubai’s policy gives you a disclosure list to hold vendors to, plus independent third-party validation.
- Overrides, appeals, monitoring against validation results and incident reporting are part of the expected set-up.
- Check DHA for a version newer than the one dated 2021, and ask your compliance team how it fits with other Dubai rules.
- This is general information, not legal advice.
