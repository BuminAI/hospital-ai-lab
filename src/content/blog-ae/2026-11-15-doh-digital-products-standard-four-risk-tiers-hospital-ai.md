A new standard from the Abu Dhabi Department of Health (DoH) changes how AI projects begin. The Digital Products Governance Standard (reference DoH/ST/DHS/DPGS/V1/26) was published in March 2026 and takes effect in August 2026. It does not mention AI as an afterthought: its definition of digital products includes systems, applications, collaborative platforms, electronic health records, telemedicine and wearable devices powered by technologies such as robotics, IoT, 5G, blockchain, artificial intelligence, augmented reality and virtual reality. It applies to all DoH stakeholders that develop and manage digital products.

## Two named people, one assessment

Each digital product needs at least a Digital Product Owner and a Digital Product Architect, or equivalents. They may be dedicated roles or added to existing duties. Before any digital product starts, a feasibility study and risk assessment must be done. Products that DoH requires to be reviewed follow the DoH ADHTAC approval and the web and mobile application approval.

For an administrator this is practical: when a department proposes an AI tool, ask who the owner and architect are, and where the feasibility study and risk assessment are.

## The four tiers

The standard sorts products by risk:

- **Very high risk:** prohibited from deployment.
- **High risk:** conformity assessments before and after deployment, in coordination with the DoH Data and Digital Governance Office.
- **Medium risk:** governance policies, standards and risk reporting.
- **Low and very low risk:** exempt from formal risk reporting, but their controls must be monitored for effectiveness.

Risk assessments must be documented in a risk register with defined ownership. The standard does not say which tier a given product falls in; that comes from the assessment, so record how your hospital reached its answer.

## Privacy for patient-facing products

A patient-facing product needs a privacy policy, a privacy notice shown at registration or before download with affirmative consent such as an “I Agree” box, a separate affirmative consent for sharing data for a specific purpose that names the recipients and purposes, published procedures for security incidents and breaches, instructions to opt out or withdraw consent, and records of consents. Products developed by or owned by DoH, or marked for government use, must integrate UAE Pass for authentication.

## Key takeaways

- The standard covers AI-powered digital products in Abu Dhabi and applies from August 2026.
- Name an owner and an architect, and do a feasibility study and risk assessment before the project starts.
- Know the four tiers; keep a risk register with owners.
- This is general information, not legal advice. Confirm applicability with your compliance team.
