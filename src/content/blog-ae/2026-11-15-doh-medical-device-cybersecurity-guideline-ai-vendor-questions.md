On the wards, many devices now run software and sit on the hospital network. The Department of Health (DoH) in Abu Dhabi published its Medical Device Cybersecurity in Healthcare Facilities Guideline (reference DOH/GL/0000002397/24) in November 2026. It takes effect in January 2027 and lists October 2029 as its revision date.

## What it covers and who it applies to

The guideline covers standalone medical device software, for example diagnostic or therapeutic applications, and software embedded in hardware such as MRI machines, infusion pumps and ventilators. It applies to implantable, mobile, software-only and network-connected devices. It applies to healthcare facilities, providers, IT departments, biomedical and clinical engineers, distributors and vendors, and manufacturers.

## What vendors are asked to provide

Manufacturers, distributors and vendors are required to integrate security-by-design, conduct pre- and post-market cybersecurity risk assessments, provide transparent documentation including Manufacturer Disclosure Statements for Medical Device Security (MDS2), deliver timely updates and patches, keep clear channels for security alerts and recalls, and secure the supply chain.

So, in a vendor meeting, ask:

1. Can we have your MDS2 for this product and version?
2. How are patches delivered, and how fast after a vulnerability is found?
3. Where do security alerts and recalls reach us?
4. How do you secure your own supply chain?

## What hospitals are asked to do

The guideline asks hospitals to keep an inventory of all medical devices with software and firmware versions, operating system, IP and MAC addresses and network configuration; assess threats and vulnerabilities with the STRIDE method; segment networks; apply software updates and patches promptly; keep backup and recovery plans; run incident response plans with drills; and handle decommissioning securely, including data wiping or physical destruction.

## Reporting

For any cybersecurity information or incident involving the Internet of Medical Things, including vulnerabilities or breaches, a healthcare entity must report to the Abu Dhabi Healthcare Cybersecurity Emergency Response Team (ADHCCERT) immediately upon discovery and without delay. Adverse events related to medical devices, including those linked to software or patch updates, go without delay to the DoH Pharmacovigilance team under the medical device reporting standard.

Any new medical device, or an existing one with cybersecurity considerations, intended for use in Abu Dhabi healthcare facilities must be evaluated by DoH’s Health Technology Assessment department before implementation.

## Key takeaways

- The guideline takes effect in January 2027; ask AI and device vendors for their MDS2 and patch and recall process now.
- Keep a device inventory with software versions; report connected-device cyber incidents to ADHCCERT immediately.
- New devices need a DoH Health Technology Assessment evaluation before implementation.
