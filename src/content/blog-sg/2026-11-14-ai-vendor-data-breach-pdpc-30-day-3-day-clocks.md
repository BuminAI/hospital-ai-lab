Handing patient data to an AI vendor adds another place where a breach can start. Hospital administrators and IT leads should know the timing before the first incident, not during it. The Personal Data Protection Commission (PDPC) publishes a Guide on Managing and Notifying Data Breaches under the PDPA. The version read for this article was revised on 15 March 2021; check the PDPC website for later revisions.

## The two clocks

According to the guide, once an organisation has credible grounds to believe a data breach has occurred, whether through self-discovery, an alert from the public or a notification from its data intermediary, it must take reasonable and expeditious steps to assess whether the breach is notifiable. That assessment should be completed within 30 calendar days, and every step must be documented. Once the organisation determines that the breach is notifiable, it must notify the PDPC within 3 calendar days.

## What makes a breach notifiable

The guide describes two grounds. A breach is notifiable if it is likely to result in significant harm to the affected individuals, or if it is of significant scale. Significant scale means the personal data of 500 or more individuals. In that case the organisation must notify the PDPC even if no prescribed personal data is involved. If the exact number is unknown, the organisation should notify when it has reason to believe that at least 500 individuals are affected.

## Who else hears about it

The Ministry of Health’s AI guidelines (AIHGle 2.0) tell hospitals to set up internal reporting and to follow existing frameworks: data-related incidents go to the PDPC, adverse events from the use of medical devices to HSA, and critical cybersecurity incidents to the Cyber Security Agency of Singapore. They also say contingency plans should include shutting the AI solution down and switching to a protocol that does not involve it.

## What to put in the vendor contract

These are the author’s suggestions, not requirements:

- A duty to tell the hospital quickly, with a named contact who answers at night, so the hospital’s own assessment can start.
- A list of sub-processors, such as cloud and model providers, and where the data is stored.
- Return or erasure of data at exit, including logs and backups.
- Cooperation with the hospital’s investigation.

## Key takeaways

- The PDPC guide (revised 15 March 2021) sets a 30-calendar-day assessment and a 3-calendar-day notification once a breach is determined notifiable.
- Breaches likely to cause significant harm, or involving 500 or more individuals, are notifiable.
- AIHGle 2.0 names the PDPC, HSA and the Cyber Security Agency of Singapore as reporting routes for different incidents.
- This article did not read the Personal Data Protection Act itself. It is general information, not legal advice.
