When a hospital buys an AI tool, people often talk as if there were only two parties: the vendor and the hospital. Singapore's guidance draws a finer line. The Ministry of Health (MOH) page on emerging regulatory policy issues describes version 2.0 of the Artificial Intelligence in Healthcare Guidelines (AIHGle 2.0), updated jointly by MOH and the Health Sciences Authority (HSA). This post reads only that web page, not the full guidelines, so treat it as a starting point.

## Three groups, three roles

According to the MOH page, AIHGle 2.0 speaks to three stakeholder groups:

- **Developers**, for example manufacturers.
- **Deployers**, that is, healthcare organisations.
- **Users**, that is, healthcare professionals.

The page lists three key updates in version 2.0: clearer responsibilities for each stakeholder group to strengthen accountability, guidance on transparency to support informed decision-making, and updated guidance on AI deployment, such as assessing and mitigating risks. It also says AI should augment and empower healthcare professionals, and that AIHGle is a living document that will be updated from time to time.

## Why the split matters on a ward

In daily practice the hospital is usually the deployer, and nurses and doctors are users. The two are easy to blur. A deployer decides whether a tool fits the organisation and sets up the governance around it; a user works with its output at the bedside. If no one is clearly named for the deployer role, questions from users, such as "this alert looks wrong, who do I tell?", have nowhere to go. This reading is ours, not a statement on the MOH page.

## Three resources to know about

The MOH page links three documents: the updated AIHGle 2.0 itself, an infographic summarising the roles and responsibilities of developers, deployers and users, and a deployers' toolkit that the page says supports new adopters with internal governance and selecting appropriate AI solutions. We have not read the infographic or the toolkit for this post.

## What to do this week

These are our suggestions, not MOH or HSA requirements:

1. **Name the deployer owner.** Decide which committee or person speaks for the hospital as deployer for each AI tool.
2. **Read the infographic together** with the nursing and administration leads, so everyone uses the same words for the three roles.
3. **Ask the vendor which role it claims**, in writing, before the contract is signed.

## Key takeaways

- The MOH page says AIHGle 2.0, updated jointly by MOH and HSA, addresses developers, deployers (healthcare organisations) and users (healthcare professionals).
- Version 2.0 clarifies responsibilities, adds transparency guidance and updates deployment guidance, including risk assessment.
- Name a deployer owner and use the published infographic and toolkit. This is general information, not legal advice.
