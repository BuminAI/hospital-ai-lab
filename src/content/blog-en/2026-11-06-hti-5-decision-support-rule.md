If your electronic health record vendor has been telling you its AI-based decision support meets federal transparency requirements, a rule published at the end of October changes what those requirements are. It matters to hospital administrators because it moves some of the burden of vendor review from the certification program to you.

## What was published

The Federal Register shows a final rule titled Health Data, Technology, and Interoperability: ONC Deregulatory Actions To Unleash Prosperity, published October 28, 2026 at 91 FR 67916. The abstract says it focuses on deregulatory actions on health IT standards, implementation specifications and certification criteria, and on certification programs. It is effective December 28, 2026, except for three amendatory instructions that are effective January 1, 2027.

## What it does to decision support

The preamble says the rule reduces the scope of the decision support interventions certification criterion in 45 CFR 170.315(b)(11). It removes paragraphs (b)(11)(iv) through (vi): the source attribute support requirements, the source attribute access and modification requirements, and the intervention risk-management requirements for predictive decision support. The preamble describes the source attribute requirements as the "model card" requirements.

The rule also removes the older clinical decision support criterion in 170.315(a)(9). ONC points out that this criterion had already expired for certification on January 1, 2025, so its removal has no further practical effect.

ONC acknowledges comments that the change shifts evaluation and governance work to providers and health systems. It says it will consider in future rulemaking whether similar transparency requirements should be reinstated, and that applying risk management to supplied predictive tools may still be a best practice.

## What a hospital can do

Certification used to give you a baseline you could ask a vendor to document. That baseline is narrower now, so your own questions matter more:

- What data was the predictive tool trained and tested on, and how does that compare with our patients?
- How does the vendor monitor performance and drift, and what will it show us?
- Who at the hospital reviews the tool's results after go-live?

None of this is a legal requirement created by the rule. It is the practical consequence of a smaller federal checklist.

## Key takeaways

- The HTI-5 final rule (91 FR 67916) was published October 28, 2026, effective December 28, 2026, with three instructions effective January 1, 2027.
- It removes 45 CFR 170.315(b)(11)(iv) through (vi), covering source attribute and risk-management requirements for decision support.
- ONC says it will consider reinstating similar requirements in future rulemaking.
- Hospital vendor review carries more weight as the certification checklist shrinks.
