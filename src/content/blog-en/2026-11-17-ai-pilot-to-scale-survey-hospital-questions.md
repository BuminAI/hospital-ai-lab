A pilot that works on one unit does not always work across the whole hospital. Healthcare Dive reported on November 16, 2026, on a Google Cloud report about why healthcare organizations struggle to move AI from pilot to everyday use. The numbers come from a vendor-published survey, so read them as one view, not a measurement of the whole industry. Here is what was reported and how administrators and nursing leaders can use it.

## What the survey reported

According to Healthcare Dive, Google's survey covered more than 250 healthcare executives and was run by the consumer insights firm Hypothesis Group. The article does not say when it was fielded. Reported results include:

- Nearly 60% faced integration and deployment challenges moving enterprise AI from pilot to live systems.
- 40% cited unpredictable costs as a reason for pausing AI projects.
- Only 30% said they had scaled advanced or agentic AI across operations, which Healthcare Dive says is 13 percentage points below the all-industry benchmark.
- More than 80% named cost as a hurdle to relying less on existing AI vendors.
- About 40% said protecting sensitive data is an especially important AI consideration.

Respondents also said they want full visibility into how an AI tool is governed and the ability to customize it. We did not open the original report, so these figures come from the news article.

## Why pilots stall

The barriers named are practical, not scientific: connecting the tool to existing systems, costs that change as use grows, more technical complexity, and concern for sensitive data. None of these show up in a demo, which is why they surprise teams after the pilot succeeds.

## Five questions to ask before scaling

These are our suggestions, not the survey's, and they are not legal advice.

1. **What changes in the workflow?** Ask nurses on the pilot unit what they stopped doing, started doing, or worked around.
2. **What will it cost at ten times the use?** Ask the vendor how pricing changes with volume, and get it in writing.
3. **How does it connect to our systems, and who maintains that link?**
4. **Where does patient data go?** Confirm with your privacy officer what leaves the hospital and who can see it.
5. **How would we switch vendors?** The survey's cost-of-switching finding suggests asking this before signing, not after.

## Key takeaways

- A Google Cloud survey, as reported on November 16, 2026, found about 60% hit integration challenges and 30% had scaled advanced AI.
- It is a vendor-run survey of more than 250 executives; the original report was not reviewed here.
- Before scaling a pilot, check workflow changes, cost growth, system connections, data flow, and exit options. This is general information, not legal advice.
