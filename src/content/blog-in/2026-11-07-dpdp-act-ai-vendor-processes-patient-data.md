Sending patient records to an AI vendor feels like handing a task to someone else. The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 treats it differently: the hospital that decides why and how the data is processed stays responsible.

## Who is who

The Act calls the hospital a data fiduciary: a person who alone or with others decides the purpose and means of processing. A vendor that processes personal data on the hospital’s behalf is a data processor.

## Responsibility does not move

Section 8(1) says the data fiduciary is responsible for complying with the Act for any processing it undertakes, or that a data processor undertakes on its behalf, irrespective of any agreement to the contrary. A processor may be engaged to offer goods or services only under a valid contract (section 8(2)). A clause in the vendor’s standard terms that puts all risk on the hospital does not change the Act’s allocation.

## Security safeguards and breaches

Section 8(5) requires reasonable security safeguards to prevent a personal data breach, including in processing by a data processor. In the Schedule, a breach of this obligation carries a penalty that may extend to two hundred and fifty crore rupees. If a breach occurs, section 8(6) requires the data fiduciary to give the Data Protection Board and each affected person an intimation in the form and manner prescribed. Failing to give it carries a penalty that may extend to two hundred crore rupees. The deadline and form sit in the Rules, which this site could not open, so it states no deadline.

## Data used to make decisions

Where personal data is likely to be used to make a decision that affects a person, or disclosed to another data fiduciary, section 8(3) asks the data fiduciary to ensure its completeness, accuracy and consistency. A tool that scores or ranks patients on the basis of records is the obvious place to ask who is responsible for this.

## What to put in the vendor contract

1. The purposes for which the vendor may process the data, and nothing else, including whether it may use it to improve its own product.
2. Its security safeguards, described so that you can compare them with section 8(5).
3. How and how fast it tells you of a breach, and who answers at night.
4. Erasure when consent is withdrawn or the purpose ends, including by its sub-processors (section 8(7)).

## Key takeaways

- The hospital is the data fiduciary and stays responsible for what its processor does (section 8(1)).
- A processor may be engaged only under a valid contract (section 8(2)).
- Reasonable security safeguards are required, and the Schedule sets penalties up to ₹250 crore for that failure.
- A breach must be intimated to the Board and each affected person; check the Rules for the form and time.
