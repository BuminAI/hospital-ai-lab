More hospital software now produces a score, a rank or a flag about a patient: a no-show risk, a priority on a waiting list, a billing check. India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 has a short rule that applies when personal data is used this way. This post reads it in plain words. It is general information, not legal advice.

## What section 8(3) says

Where personal data is likely to be used to make a decision that affects the person, or to be disclosed to another data fiduciary, the data fiduciary must ensure its completeness, accuracy and consistency. The hospital is usually the data fiduciary.

## Why it matters for AI tools

A score is only as good as the records behind it. If an address, a date of birth or a past visit is wrong, a tool that ranks or flags patients may be wrong too. This reading is ours, not the Act's text.

## Questions to ask this week

These are our suggestions, not requirements in the Act:

1. **Which tools make or shape a decision about a patient?** List them.
2. **Which fields does each tool read?** Ask the vendor in writing.
3. **Who corrects a wrong field, and how fast?** Name the owner in the records team.

## Key takeaways

- Section 8(3) of the DPDP Act asks the data fiduciary to ensure completeness, accuracy and consistency of personal data used for decisions about the person.
- AI scores and flags depend on the records they read.
- List decision tools, the fields they use and who corrects errors. This is general information, not legal advice.

Source: [Gazette of India (Extraordinary), 11 Aug 2023 — The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (PRS copy)](https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/bills_parliament/2023/Digital_Personal_Data_Protection_Act,_2023.pdf), section 8(3).
