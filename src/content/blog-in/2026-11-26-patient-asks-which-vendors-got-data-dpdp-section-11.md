A patient asks at the front desk, “Who else has my information?” In a hospital using an appointment app, a reporting service and an AI tool, the honest answer may take several departments to assemble. India’s data protection law has a provision about this question, and the work of preparing for it can start with a spreadsheet.

## What section 11 says

Under section 11 of the [Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023](https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/bills_parliament/2023/Digital_Personal_Data_Protection_Act,_2023.pdf), a Data Principal has a right to obtain certain information from a Data Fiduciary to whom they have given consent, including consent as referred to in section 7(a). On request, made in the manner prescribed, this includes:

- a summary of the personal data being processed, and the processing activities;
- the identities of all other Data Fiduciaries and Data Processors with whom the data has been shared, with a description of the data shared;
- other related information, as may be prescribed.

There is a limit. The second and third items do not apply to sharing with another Data Fiduciary authorised by law to obtain the data, where that sharing follows a written request for preventing, detecting or investigating offences or cyber incidents, or for prosecution or punishment of offences.

## Check what is in force

Section 1(2) says the Act comes into force on dates the Central Government appoints by notification, and different provisions may start on different dates. We have not been able to read the notified rules or dates from primary sources, so this article does not say whether section 11 applies today. Ask your legal team to confirm the current position, including how a request must be made. Section 17 also lets the government exempt some classes of fiduciaries from certain provisions, section 11 among them.

## Build a vendor register now

The following is our suggestion, not a format from the Act. One row for each system or service that receives patient data:

1. **Name of the vendor and the service**
2. **What data goes to it**, in plain words (name and phone; reports; images)
3. **Why** it receives the data
4. **How it gets there** (integration, upload, email)
5. **Contract reference**; section 8(2) speaks of engaging a Data Processor under a valid contract for activities related to offering goods or services
6. **Hospital owner** who can answer questions

Remember the tools that arrived quietly: a transcription app on one doctor’s phone, a free translation website, a chatbot someone uses for letters. Ask each department to list them, and make clear that listing a tool is not a confession.

## Try it this week

Pick one patient journey, such as registration to discharge. Walk it with a nurse and someone from billing, and write down every outside service that touches the patient’s information. Then ask: if a request arrived tomorrow, who would collect the answer, and how long would it take?

## Key takeaways

- Section 11 covers a summary of data and the identities of those it was shared with, subject to limits.
- Commencement and procedure need to be confirmed with your legal team.
- A vendor register is useful preparation and can start with one patient journey.
- This is general information, not legal advice.
