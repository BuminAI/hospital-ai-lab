If your team uses AI for posts, videos or patient stories, there is a recent document to read. On 6 October 2026 the National Medical Commission (NMC) issued [Guidelines on Ethical Advertising and Public Communication](https://nmc.org.in/whats-new/download/1874) for hospitals, medical institutions and registered medical practitioners, in force with immediate effect.

In this article, "medical AI" means the broad field, "hospital AI" means AI used in hospital work, and "AI marketing" means using AI for promotion. These are working labels.

## 1. AI-made promotion counts as advertising

Para 3.2 says digital, social-media, influencer-led, search-engine and AI-generated or AI-assisted promotional communications are treated as advertising where their content or manner of dissemination is promotional.

Para 7.2 says an AI-generated promotional campaign for furtherance of commercial interest is prohibited, and that AI-generated promotional content conforming to the guidelines and applicable law must carry a source mark stating that its origin is AI. Read both sentences together with your counsel before planning an AI-made campaign.

## 2. What AI must not be used for

Under para 7.2(a) to (d):

- no misleading, deceptive or unverifiable statements about diagnosis, treatment, outcomes, qualifications, patient experiences or services
- no creating or manipulating a patient's image, testimonial, voice or clinical outcome, or a synthetic endorsement presented as a real person's
- disclosure where the artificial nature of content matters to what the audience understands
- patient information used as input to an AI system must comply with privacy, confidentiality, data protection and ethics requirements

## 3. Patient information and consent

Para 6.1 bars using patient information, photographs, records or testimonials in advertising except where law permits it and the necessary consent and safeguards are in place. Para 6.2 adds that consent alone does not make a prohibited testimonial, before-and-after depiction or success claim permissible.

## 4. Claims and reviews

Hospitals may share factual, verifiable information such as name, location, departments, facilities, services and charges (para 8.3). Claims such as "best" or "No.1" are barred unless objectively verifiable on the stated terms (para 8.1(ix)), and procuring or manipulating fake reviews, followers, likes or search rankings is prohibited (para 8.1(xii)).

The guidelines say they do not override existing statutory requirements, including the 2002 professional conduct regulations (para 2.1(f)). Digital personal data is also subject to the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 and rules; check which provisions have commenced.

## Key takeaways

- NMC's guidelines of 6 October 2026 treat AI-generated promotional communication as advertising.
- AI must not create or alter patient images, testimonials, voices or outcomes.
- AI-generated promotional content that is otherwise permitted must be marked as AI.
- This is general information, not legal advice. Read the official text and ask counsel.
