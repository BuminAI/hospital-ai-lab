A nurse manager asks the question after a vendor demo: “Are we supposed to train everyone on this?” The EU AI Act has a short answer in Article 4, and it has been in force for longer than most of the Act.

## What Article 4 says

Providers and deployers of AI systems shall take measures to ensure, to their best extent, a sufficient level of AI literacy of their staff and other persons dealing with the operation and use of AI systems on their behalf. The measures should take into account their technical knowledge, experience, education and training, the context in which the systems are used, and the people on whom the systems are used.

Three points stand out for a hospital:

- It applies to **deployers**, which includes a hospital using a vendor’s system under its authority.
- It reaches **other persons acting on your behalf**, not only employees. Think agency staff or contractors.
- It is scaled to **context and role**: “to their best extent” and “taking into account” are the Act’s own words. It does not set a syllabus or an exam.

## Since when?

Article 113(a) says Chapters I and II of the Regulation apply from 2 February 2025, and Article 4 is in Chapter I. That is earlier than the general application date of 2 August 2026, as published. We did not read later EU amendments, so check the current text.

## The Irish policy angle

The Department of Health’s AI for Care strategy also commits to building AI literacy across the health service and among patients and service users, under its “human in the loop” principle. HIQA’s draft guidance names training under its responsiveness principle. Neither document sets a course; both treat literacy as part of using AI safely.

## A simple starting plan

1. **List who touches each AI tool:** clinicians, nurses, administrators, IT, front-desk staff and agency workers.
2. **Write a one-page brief per tool:** what it is for, what it must not be used for, how to override it, and how to report a problem.
3. **Match the depth to the role:** a nurse who reads its alerts needs different material from the person who configures it.
4. **Record it:** who was briefed and when, and re-brief after significant updates.

This plan is the author’s suggestion, not a requirement of Article 4.

## Key takeaways

- Article 4 asks deployers to ensure a sufficient level of AI literacy among staff and others who operate or use AI systems for them.
- It applies from 2 February 2025 as published, and it is scaled to knowledge, role and context.
- Ireland’s AI for Care strategy and HIQA’s draft guidance point the same way. This is general information, not legal advice.
