At 2am on a ward, an infusion pump or a monitoring app misbehaves after an update. Who does the nurse in charge tell, and what happens next? HPRA’s pages for hospitals answer both questions, and the Medical Device Regulation explains the manufacturer’s side.

## What HPRA asks hospitals to report

HPRA says hospitals and healthcare professionals should report serious incidents. A serious incident is any incident that directly or indirectly led, might have led or might lead to the death of a patient, user or other person, the temporary or permanent serious deterioration of someone’s state of health, or a serious public health threat. If you are in doubt, HPRA encourages you to report anyway.

You can report through HPRA’s online report form, by emailing a completed incident report form to devicesafety@hpra.ie, by post to the Medical Devices Department, or by phone on +353 (0)1 676 4971. HPRA’s page sets no deadline for hospitals, so decide your own internal target and write it on the incident card.

## The manufacturer’s clocks

Under Article 87 of the MDR, manufacturers report a serious incident immediately after establishing a causal relationship, and not later than 15 days after becoming aware. For a serious public health threat the limit is 2 days, and for death or an unanticipated serious deterioration in health it is 10 days. Article 87(10) asks Member States to encourage and enable healthcare professionals, users and patients to report suspected serious incidents. These are the manufacturer’s deadlines, not the hospital’s.

## When the notice comes from the manufacturer

A manufacturer may send a Field Safety Notice (FSN) describing a Field Safety Corrective Action (FSCA). HPRA lists software updates among examples, alongside changes to instructions for use, repair, recall or replacement. HPRA says each hospital manages these under its own quality or risk management protocols, and recommends six steps:

1. **Receive and notify:** a documented process to receive notices and pass them to clinical, technical and procurement staff.
2. **Assess applicability:** do we hold affected devices? Traceability is essential.
3. **Assess risk:** if devices have been used, are patients at risk or in need of follow-up?
4. **Implement** the manufacturer’s instructions.
5. **Document** receipt, devices identified, actions, dates and people, and return acknowledgements.
6. **Report** incidents to HPRA where required.

## Where AI fits

If the AI-enabled product is a device, these routes apply to its software updates too. If it is a high-risk AI system under the AI Act, a deployer also informs the provider first and then the authorities of a serious incident (Article 26(5)).

## Key takeaways

- HPRA asks hospitals to report serious incidents (death, serious deterioration in health, serious public health threat), and encourages reporting if in doubt.
- Manufacturers have 15, 10 or 2-day clocks under the MDR; HPRA sets none for hospitals, so set your own.
- Treat software updates as possible field safety actions, with a six-step routine. This is general information, not legal advice.
