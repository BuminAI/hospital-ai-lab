When a vendor says its product “fits the national strategy”, it helps to know what the strategy actually says. The Department of Health’s AI for Care strategy for 2026-2030 is the Irish document to read. It is a policy strategy, not legislation, and the copy we read carries no publication date. Its foreword is signed by the Minister for Health, Jennifer Carroll MacNeill.

## Four pillars

The strategy sets four strategic pillars: AI for clinical care, AI for operations, AI for research and innovation, and AI for public health. For a hospital administrator the operations pillar is the one closest to daily work, but the same governance questions apply to all four.

## Six guiding principles

These are the strategy’s principles, which it says must be referenced throughout all AI efforts:

1. **Person-centred:** health outcomes and the needs of the public are central to decisions about adopting AI.
2. **Transparent and trustworthy:** people are told how, when and where AI is used to support healthcare decisions and what impact it might have.
3. **Human in the loop:** AI supports clinicians, who stay at the centre of care. The strategy also commits to building AI literacy across the health service and among patients.
4. **Lived experience:** a continuous learning approach that involves patients and clinicians.
5. **Governance and safety:** AI operates within governance that aligns with health policy, regulation and ethics, including data protection requirements and AI-specific legislation.
6. **Proven benefit:** a commitment to measurable value through monitoring and evaluation.

## How it connects to other documents

The strategy says the HSE has developed a corresponding AI Implementation Framework, and that HIQA’s forthcoming national guidance will support compliance with the EU AI Act. HIQA has since published that guidance (29 July 2026). We have not read the HSE framework.

## Using it in a vendor meeting

Turn the principles into questions:

- **Transparent:** how will patients be told, and who explains it?
- **Human in the loop:** who can override, and are they trained?
- **Governance and safety:** which rules apply, and where is the record?
- **Proven benefit:** what will we measure, and who looks at it?

A strategy is not a compliance certificate. Use it to frame a proposal, and use the binding documents (the AI Act, the GDPR, the Medical Device Regulation) to test it.

## Key takeaways

- AI for Care 2026-2030 sets four pillars and six guiding principles; it is a strategy, not a set of rules.
- It stresses keeping a human in the loop, transparency and AI literacy.
- Use it to frame vendor questions, not to prove compliance. This is general information, not legal advice.
