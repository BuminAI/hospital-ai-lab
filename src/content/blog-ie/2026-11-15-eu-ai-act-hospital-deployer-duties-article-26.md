Many hospitals assume the AI Act is the vendor’s problem. It is not entirely. The Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 applies directly in Ireland, and it gives separate duties to “deployers”: anyone using an AI system under their authority, except in a personal non-professional activity. A hospital using a vendor’s AI product is a deployer.

## When does it apply?

As published in the Official Journal on 12 July 2024, Article 113 says the Regulation applies from 2 August 2026, with exceptions: Chapters I and II (general provisions and prohibited practices) from 2 February 2025, and Article 6(1) with its corresponding obligations from 2 August 2027. We read the text as published; EU amendments after that were not read, so check the current version.

## Which systems are “high-risk”?

Article 26 applies to high-risk systems. Two routes matter to hospitals. Under Article 6(1), an AI system that is a product, or a safety component of a product, covered by the EU product laws in Annex I (which include the Medical Device Regulation and the In Vitro Diagnostic Regulation), and that needs third-party conformity assessment, is high-risk. Under Annex III, point 5, uses include evaluating eligibility for public healthcare services and emergency healthcare patient triage systems.

## What a deployer must do (Article 26)

- Use the system according to its **instructions for use**.
- Assign **human oversight** to people with the necessary competence, training, authority and support.
- Where you control the input data, make sure it is **relevant and sufficiently representative**.
- **Monitor** the system. If you think it may present a risk, inform the provider or distributor and the market surveillance authority, and suspend use.
- For a **serious incident**, inform the provider first, then the importer or distributor and the authorities.
- Keep the **logs** you control for at least six months, unless other law says otherwise.
- Before using a high-risk system at work, **tell workers** and their representatives.
- Use the provider’s information to help with a **data protection impact assessment** where applicable.

## What to do now

Ask each vendor three things in writing: do you consider this product high-risk, and under which provision; what do your instructions for use say about oversight; and what logs does the system keep and who controls them. Then name an overseer and put the incident route on a card.

## Key takeaways

- A hospital that uses an AI system under its authority is a deployer with its own duties.
- Article 26 covers instructions, oversight, input data, monitoring, incidents, logs, informing workers and impact assessments.
- Dates are as published: general application 2 August 2026, Article 6(1) from 2 August 2027. Check for amendments. This is general information, not legal advice.
